## Receptors
- **Pacinian**: deep, onion-like, high-frequency vibration, **rapidly adapting**
- **Meissner**: light touch, flutter, low-frequency vibration, **rapidly adapting**
- **Merkel**: static touch, light pressure, texture, **slowly adapting**
- **Ruffini**: skin stretch, **slowly adapting**
- **Hair follicle**: detects hair movement
- **Muscle spindle**: muscle stretch, **parallel**, **Ia/II**
- **Golgi tendon organ**: muscle tension, **series**, **Ib**

## Concepts
- **generator potential**: in nerve ending
- **receptor potential**: in receptor cell
- bigger stimulus → bigger receptor potential → **higher AP frequency**
- **RA** receptors detect change
- **SA** receptors detect sustained stimulus
- **small receptive fields** = better localisation
- **two-point discrimination** depends on field size + receptor density
- **lateral inhibition** sharpens localisation

## Pathways
- **DCML**: fine touch / discriminative sensation pathway; crosses in brainstem
- **Spinothalamic**: pain, temperature, simple tactile; crosses in spinal cord

### Clinical
## If the patient loses...

- **high-frequency vibration** → think **Pacinian corpuscles**
- **light touch / flutter / low-frequency vibration** → think **Meissner’s corpuscles**
- **static touch / pressure / texture discrimination** → think **Merkel disks**
- **skin stretch** → think **Ruffini endings**
- **hair movement detection** → think **hair follicle receptors**
- **muscle stretch / limb position** → think **muscle spindle**
- **muscle tension / force** → think **Golgi tendon organ**

## Questions

1) Sensory receptors act as transducers, converting sensory information into a digital code.

What sensory receptor characteristic determines the digital code?
**Frequency of action potential**

2) A 65-year-old man presents with progressive loss of vibration and proprioception in both feet but pain and temperature sensation are preserved. He reports difficulty walking in the dark and frequently trips when climbing stairs.

Which sensory receptor is primarily responsible for detecting the affected sensory modality?
**Pacinian corpuscles**. They detect high-frequency vibration and deep pressure. A loss of these receptors causes impaired balance and position sense.

3) You are on placement at a GP practice and are performing a neurological examination of the upper limb on a 44 year old female patient. She is attending the GP after burning herself due to not being able to feel the hot items when cooking. 

Everything is normal except the patient is unable to correctly identify the sharp or dull sensation when you use the neurotip.

What part of her nervous system is most likely causing these problems?
This patient is unable to sense pain/pin-prick and temperature. These sensations are carried up the **spinothalamic tract**.

4) A 40-year-old construction builder presents with concerns after noticing they cannot distinguish between different grades of sandpaper - unable to distinguish between fine and coarse. His are deeply weathered, thick, rough, and frequent damage has led to coarse scars, due to the physical toll of construction work. 

An examination is performed - he struggles to distinguish textures when his eyes are closed, despite normal vibration perception.

Which receptor pairing is most likely to be affected?
- **Merkel disks** are slowly adapting, have small receptive fields, and encode edges, shape, and fine detail during sustained contact.
- **Meissner corpuscles** are rapidly adapting and respond to skin motion and low-frequency vibration as the finger moves across a surface.
